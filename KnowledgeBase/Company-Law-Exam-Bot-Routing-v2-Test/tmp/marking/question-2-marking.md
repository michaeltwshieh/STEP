## 2.1.1 - enforceability of the pre-incorporation contract: 6/6

### Marks gained

1. **Company did not exist and cannot contract at common law.** The answer begins with the decisive fact and rule: "Company Ltd did not exist" and therefore "lacked legal capacity to contract" (answer line 7). This earns the core KAP point that a pre-incorporation contract is unenforceable by or against the company.
2. **Both enforcement directions are expressly applied.** It directly answers both scenarios: the Company cannot compel Sotheby's to deliver, and Sotheby's cannot enforce payment against the Company (line 7). This is stronger than giving the rule abstractly.
3. **No common-law ratification after incorporation.** The answer correctly states that incorporation does not cure the defect and the Company cannot ratify the earlier agreement (line 7), matching both Module 3 and the KAP.
4. **Promoter's personal exposure is identified and applied.** It cites *Kelner v Baxter* and explains that Ms AB is personally bound, so Sotheby's may pursue her for the GBP 1.5 million price (line 9). This captures the KAP point that the promoter must perform or pay compensation for breach.
5. **Novation is correctly distinguished from ratification/adoption.** The answer explains that a new contract between Ms AB, Sotheby's and the Company requires all three parties' consent (line 9). This is the correct common-law route and earns the KAP's novation point.
6. **Chosen-jurisdiction statutory comparison is complete.** The preliminary paragraph selects The Bahamas (line 3), and the answer consistently applies section 70 of the International Business Companies Act 2000. It states that the Company may adopt without Sotheby's consent and then assumes both the right to delivery and liability for the price (line 11). It also recognises that the promoter-discharge consequence must be checked rather than asserted beyond the local source wording.

### Marks lost

**None.** All credit-bearing KAP concepts are present and applied to the facts.

### Caution not warranting a deduction

- **Unsupported/overclaim risk:** "The better view is that she is personally bound and entitled" and that AB herself could enforce delivery (line 9) goes beyond the KAP, which expressly states personal liability but does not separately articulate the promoter's positive enforcement right. It is a plausible reciprocal-contract consequence on these facts, and it does not displace the correct answer that the Company cannot enforce, so no mark is removed. A tighter exam answer would distinguish the certain KAP proposition (AB's liability) from any fact-sensitive question whether the contract is construed as giving AB personal enforcement rights.

### Extra sophistication that does not generate additional marks

- Identifying the contract value, the bilateral practical consequences after adoption, and the precise uncertainty over automatic statutory discharge are useful sophistication, but the part remains capped at 6.

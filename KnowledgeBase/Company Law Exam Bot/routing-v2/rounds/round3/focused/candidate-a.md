## MCQ11

**Question:** The company will move its registered office to a new street address within the same jurisdiction. Which route must be opened first?

**Polarity:** correct - the qualifier "must" asks for the required opening route, and "first" identifies the initial address-move step. The supplied jurisdiction fact is that the address remains within the existing jurisdiction; the regime is otherwise unspecified, the relevant actor is the board/director, and the stage is the pre-notification office move.

**Answer:** D - Module 5 section 3.2, Module 10 section 5.4, and Appendix 12 address-move resolution.

**Why:** Module 5 section 3.2 states that moving the registered office within the state is not an alteration of the memorandum and sets the corporate procedure as a board resolution followed by notification to the registrar within 14 days. Module 10 section 5.4 and Appendix 12 match that director-resolution and notification route.

**Option A:** refuted - public-to-private re-registration concerns a change in the company's nature, not a street-address move; the governing passage instead requires the board address resolution and registrar notification.

**Option B:** refuted - migration is the route for changing the company's nationality or jurisdiction of domicile, whereas the facts expressly keep the address within the same jurisdiction.

**Option C:** refuted - reinstatement is a later restoration stage after removal; nothing in the facts indicates that the company has been struck off or dissolved, and it does not address an office move.

**Option D:** supported - it identifies the same-jurisdiction registered-office rule, its procedural overlay, and the matching board-resolution precedent.

**Closest two:** B and D - the outcome-changing distinction is cross-jurisdiction migration versus a street-address change within the existing jurisdiction.

**Sources used:** Module 5, sections 2.2 and 3.2 (`Course-Manual-Module-05-The-Companys-Constitution.md`); Module 10, section 5.4 (`Course-Manual-Module-10-Company-Decision-making-Procedures.md`); Appendix 12 (`Appendix-12-Board-resolution-to-change-location-of-registered-office.md`).

**Cross-check:** Migration/re-domiciliation was discarded because no jurisdiction change is supplied; re-registration and reinstatement were discarded because no company-status change or prior removal is supplied.

**Confidence:** high (9/10).

## MCQ15

**Question:** The board appoints an officer to maintain statutory registers, prepare notices and agendas, and take minutes. Which pairing is the only correct one?

**Polarity:** correct - the qualifier "only" requires the sole correct officer/document pairing. The actor appointing is the board, the officer's stage is appointment with delegated administrative responsibilities, and the jurisdiction/regime are not further specified.

**Answer:** B - Company secretary; Appendix 24A, with Appendix 24C if the delegated role description is required.

**Why:** Module 9 sections 1.3 and 1.4.2 identify the company secretary as the chief administrative officer, with duties including statutory registers, meeting notices/agendas and minutes, and explain that the board commonly delegates those responsibilities. Appendix 24A appoints the secretary and approves the delegation, while Appendix 24C contains the corresponding role description.

**Option A:** refuted - the duties in the stem are the secretary's administrative role, and Appendix 24A is expressly the director's resolution appointing a secretary; it is not the registered-agent pairing.

**Option B:** supported - it identifies the officer whose duties match every item in the stem and correctly makes the role description conditional on delegation being documented.

**Option C:** refuted - the stem describes an administrative secretary, not a corporate director; the director-appointment document family therefore mismatches both actor and stage.

**Option D:** refuted - the stem does not concern a corporate representative or shareholder/meeting-representation documents; the secretary appointment and delegated-role documents are the matching pair.

**Closest two:** A and B - the outcome-changing distinction is that the named administrative duties and Appendix 24A/24C are secretary-specific, even though a registered agent may have separate record or compliance functions in some regimes.

**Sources used:** Module 9, sections 1.2.1, 1.3 and 1.4.2 (`Course-Manual-Module-09-Other-Officers-Secretary-and-Registered-Agent.md`); Appendix 24A (`Appendix-24A-Directors-resolution-appointing-a-secretary.md`); Appendix 24C (`Appendix-24C-Role-and-responsibilities-of-secretary.md`).

**Cross-check:** The registered-agent route was discarded because the selected duties and documents are secretary-specific; the Module 10 board-procedure overlay was considered but does not change this officer/document pairing.

**Confidence:** high (9/10).

## MCQ17

**Question:** A solvent company has assets to realise and liabilities to pay before surplus can be returned to members. Which route may properly be initiated to terminate it?

**Polarity:** correct - the qualifier "may" asks for a route that is legally available to initiate termination. The company is solvent but not yet in a zero-asset/zero-liability state; the initial actor is the directors, followed by the members and liquidator, and the stage is commencement of a formal voluntary liquidation.

**Answer:** C - Members' voluntary winding-up beginning with Appendices 30A and 30B and continuing through the applicable notices/final-account chain.

**Why:** Module 12 sections 3.1.1-3.1.2 describe members' voluntary winding-up where members no longer require the company and its assets are to be distributed after debts and liabilities are discharged. The procedure starts with the directors' declaration of solvency and assets/liabilities statement (Appendix 30A), then the members' special resolution appointing the liquidator (Appendix 30B), followed by liquidation, payment of debts, the final account and dissolution; section 4 confirms that surplus is returned only after creditors are paid.

**Option A:** refuted - voluntary striking off is designed for a zero-state company with no assets and no debts, liabilities or outstanding claims; this company still has assets to realise and liabilities to pay.

**Option B:** refuted - administrative reinstatement addresses restoration after a company has already been removed from the register, not initial termination of a solvent company still holding assets and owing liabilities.

**Option C:** supported - it follows the solvent liquidation sequence: declaration of solvency, members' decision and liquidator appointment, then the notices and completion account needed to realise assets, pay liabilities and distribute any surplus.

**Option D:** refuted - compulsory winding-up is imposed by court order, while the members' wish to end a solvent company is the voluntary-winding-up situation described in Module 12.

**Closest two:** A and C - the outcome-changing distinction is the company's non-zero state (assets and liabilities remain), which requires formal liquidation rather than zero-state striking off.

**Sources used:** Module 12, sections 2, 3.1.1-3.1.2, 4 and 6.2 (`Course-Manual-Module-12-Termination-of-Companies.md`); Appendix 30A (`Appendix-30A-Directors-declaration-of-solvency.md`); Appendix 30B (`Appendix-30B-Members-resolution-to-voluntarily-wind-up-the-company.md`).

**Cross-check:** Module 10 meeting-procedure material was considered but does not change the route choice; Module 12 section 6.2 was incorporated only to test the striking-off alternative's zero-state condition.

**Confidence:** high (9/10).

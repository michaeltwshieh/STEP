## MCQ11

**Stem:** The company will move its registered office to a new street address within the same jurisdiction. Which route must be opened first?

**Answer:** D - Module 5 section 3.2, Module 10 section 5.4, and Appendix 12 address-move resolution.

**Polarity:** correct - select the route that must be opened first.

**Why:** Module 5, §3.2 states that moving the registered office within the state of incorporation is not an alteration to the memorandum; the usual procedure is a board resolution followed by notice to the registrar. Module 10, §5.4 gives the same-jurisdiction route, including the board resolution and the registrar return, and Appendix 12 is the matching address-change resolution. That makes D the only route fitted to the supplied fact.

**Option A:** refuted - public-to-private re-registration changes the nature of the company; the stem gives only an address change within the existing jurisdiction.

**Option B:** refuted - migration is the route for changing domicile/jurisdiction. The same-jurisdiction office move does not alter the company's nationality and is expressly distinguished from formal migration in Module 5, §3.2.

**Option C:** refuted - reinstatement restores a company that has been removed from the register; nothing in the stem says this active company was struck off or dissolved.

**Option D:** supported - it identifies the same-jurisdiction registered-office procedure, its corporate procedure, and the matching board-resolution precedent.

**Closest two:** B and D - the outcome-changing distinction is whether the registered-office move changes jurisdiction/domicile. A street-address move within the same jurisdiction follows D; a move to another jurisdiction would activate the migration route in B.

**Sources used:** `Course-Manual-Module-05-The-Companys-Constitution.md`, §3.2; `Course-Manual-Module-10-Company-Decision-making-Procedures.md`, §5.4; `Appendix-12-Board-resolution-to-change-location-of-registered-office.md`.

**Cross-check:** Module 4 §2 migration route -> considered and discarded because the supplied fact is expressly within the same jurisdiction; it would not change the answer letter or confidence.

**Confidence:** high (10/10) - the exact same-jurisdiction rule, procedure, and matching precedent determine the answer.

## MCQ15

**Stem:** The board appoints an officer to maintain statutory registers, prepare notices and agendas, and take minutes. Which pairing is the only correct one?

**Answer:** B - Company secretary; Appendix 24A, with Appendix 24C if the delegated role description is required.

**Polarity:** correct - identify the only fully correct officer/precedent pairing.

**Why:** Module 9, §§1.2.1 and 1.3-1.4.2 identify the secretary as the administrative officer responsible for statutory registers, filings, meeting support and minutes, and explain that the directors delegate responsibilities to the secretary. Appendix 24A appoints the secretary and approves/delegates the role description; Appendix 24C lists the stated register, notice, agenda and minute responsibilities. Therefore B is the only pairing that matches both the actor and the documents.

**Option A:** refuted - the stated register, notice, agenda and minute functions identify the company secretary, while Appendix 24A is expressly a secretary-appointment resolution, not a registered-agent appointment.

**Option C:** refuted - a corporate director is a different office and the director-consent/first-director appointment appendices do not document the secretarial administrative role described in the stem.

**Option D:** refuted - Appendices 25H and 25J concern a corporate shareholder's representative at a members' meeting, not an officer appointed to maintain registers and service board/general-meeting administration.

**Closest two:** A and B - the outcome-changing distinction is the officer's capacity: the described statutory-register and meeting/minute administration is the company secretary's delegated role, not a registered-agent or meeting-representative function.

**Sources used:** `Course-Manual-Module-09-Other-Officers-Secretary-and-Registered-Agent.md`, §§1.2.1, 1.3, 1.4.2; `Appendix-24A-Directors-resolution-appointing-a-secretary.md`; `Appendix-24C-Role-and-responsibilities-of-secretary.md`.

## MCQ17

**Stem:** A solvent company has assets to realise and liabilities to pay before surplus can be returned to members. Which route may properly be initiated to terminate it?

**Answer:** C - Members' voluntary winding-up beginning with Appendices 30A and 30B and continuing through the applicable notices/final-account chain.

**Polarity:** correct - choose the route that may properly be initiated on the stated facts.

**Why:** Module 12, §3.1.1 describes the members' voluntary route where members no longer require the company and its assets, after debts and liabilities are discharged, are to be distributed to members. Under §3.1.2, the directors first make a declaration of solvency, then the members pass the winding-up resolution and appoint the liquidator, followed by the notices, meetings and final account. Appendix 30A supplies the solvency declaration with the assets/liabilities statement, and Appendix 30B supplies the members' winding-up and liquidator-appointment resolution. This is exactly the route described in C.

**Option A:** refuted - voluntary striking off requires a statutory zero state, including no assets and no debts or liabilities. The stem expressly says that assets remain to be realised and liabilities remain to be paid.

**Option B:** refuted - administrative reinstatement is a restoration route after removal from the register, not an initiating termination route for a solvent company that is still being wound up.

**Option C:** supported - it starts with the solvency declaration and members' resolution/liquidator appointment, then follows the liquidation notices and final-account steps supported by Module 12, §3.1.2.

**Option D:** refuted - the facts support a voluntary solvent liquidation; compulsory winding-up is not properly initiated solely because members no longer want the company, and the option omits the court-based compulsory route.

**Closest two:** A and C - the outcome-changing distinction is the company's state: striking off is for a zero-state company with no assets or liabilities, whereas the stated remaining assets and liabilities require members' voluntary winding-up so a liquidator can realise assets, pay debts and return any surplus.

**Sources used:** `Course-Manual-Module-12-Termination-of-Companies.md`, §§3.1.1, 3.1.2 and 6.2; `Appendix-30A-Directors-declaration-of-solvency.md`; `Appendix-30B-Members-resolution-to-voluntarily-wind-up-the-company.md`.

**Cross-check:** Module 12 §6.2 -> incorporated for the zero-state distinction against A; Module 10 §§2-4 -> considered but not incorporated because the question asks which termination route may be initiated, not the separate meeting mechanics.

**Confidence:** high (10/10) - the exact grounds, commencement sequence and solvency/asset-liability documents determine C.

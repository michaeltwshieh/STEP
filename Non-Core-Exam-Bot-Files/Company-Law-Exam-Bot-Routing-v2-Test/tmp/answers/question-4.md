Preliminary assumption: this answer applies the law of the British Virgin Islands and the BVI Business Companies Act 2004. Company's memorandum and articles, the XYZ Private Equity Partnership agreement and the proposed assignment have not been supplied. Their restrictions, consent requirements and execution provisions must be checked before completion.

4.1

CSP (Directors) Ltd is a corporate director, but that does not dilute its obligations. Section 120 of the BVI Business Companies Act 2004 requires Sole Director to act honestly, in good faith and in what it believes to be Company's best interests. The duty is owed to Company, rather than to CSP Ltd, CSP (Nominees) Ltd or any one beneficial owner. Miss Red's wish to acquire the XYZ interest for $3 million cannot therefore be treated as an instruction that Sole Director may follow without thought.

The fiduciary duty also requires Sole Director to use its powers for their proper purpose and exercise independent judgement. It must be loyal to Company, avoid an unauthorised conflict and neither obtain a secret profit nor accept a third-party benefit. Miss Red is a beneficial owner and the proposed purchaser, but she is not stated to be a director and no personal interest of CSP (Directors) Ltd is stated. The transaction does not by itself engage the director self-dealing rule. If an undisclosed connection, fee or benefit does exist, it must be disclosed under section 124 and the relevant articles before any decision is taken.

Section 122 imposes the care, skill and diligence of a reasonable director in the same circumstances, taking account of the nature of Company, the nature of this decision and Sole Director's position and responsibilities. The facts put Sole Director on notice. The XYZ Partnership's value depends heavily on a majority holding in Old Industrial Co Ltd, whose private shares are extremely difficult to value. Approving $3 million without examining the partnership agreement, current accounts, Old Industrial's finances, valuation method, comparable transactions and the full assignment terms would expose Sole Director to a claim for any resulting shortfall. Bishopsgate Investment Management Ltd v Maxwell (No.2) [1994] illustrates the danger: a director who signed away company assets without enquiry was liable for the loss.

Sole Director should commission an independent valuation from a suitably qualified adviser and test its assumptions. Section 123 permits reliance on records and expert advice only where Sole Director acts in good faith, makes proper enquiry when the circumstances call for it and has no knowledge that reliance is unwarranted. The known difficulty in valuing Old Industrial calls for questions, possibly a second opinion and a record of why $3 million is fair to Company. An informed commercial risk which later turns out badly is different from careless approval.

Fully informed prior approval by Company's member gives further protection. All material facts should be disclosed, including Miss Red's position, the valuation difficulty, the advice received, any range of values, Sole Director's reservations and the draft assignment. CSP (Nominees) Ltd is the sole registered member and passes Company's resolution; Mr Green, Miss Red and Mrs Brown are beneficial owners and do not themselves pass it. Since the nominee holds one share for each, it should obtain and retain their instructions under the nominee arrangements, with the benefit to Miss Red made plain. Prior approval may release Sole Director from liability to Company for a negligent or conflicted transaction. It cannot validate dishonesty, fraud or an expropriation of Company's asset prejudicing other owners or creditors. Pavlides v Jensen [1956] supports majority approval of a negligent sale at an undervalue where there was no fraud, but the rule cuts off at fraudulent self-benefit.

Sole Director can comply by obtaining the valuation and terms, making the disclosures, securing valid informed member approval and then reaching its own recorded decision. If the material does not support $3 million, or suggests that the proposal strips value from Company for Miss Red, Sole Director must refuse to proceed despite any pressure from CSP Ltd or a beneficial owner.

4.2

Section 109 of the BVI Business Companies Act 2004 places management of Company's business and affairs with its directors. The actual articles decide whether members may give a binding direction. Under Quin & Axtens Ltd v Salmon [1909], a member cannot usurp a management power vested exclusively in the board. If Company's articles contain a reserved-power provision, a separate special resolution based on that article may direct Sole Director to enter the assignment. That provision cannot be assumed here. The fully informed prior approval below addresses liability; Sole Director must still pass its own resolution.

The papers circulated before either Company resolution should include the independent valuation report, supporting financial information, the partnership agreement, all required partner or general-partner consents and the draft assignment. CSP (Nominees) Ltd should first obtain the three beneficial owners' written instructions under their nominee arrangements. As the registered member is corporate, its own board must authorise a human signatory. CSP (Directors) Ltd must do the same before its human signatory acts for it as Sole Director.

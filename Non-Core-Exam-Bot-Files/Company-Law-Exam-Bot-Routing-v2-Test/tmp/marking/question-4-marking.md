### Marks lost: complete classification

1. **Procedure/threshold loss: 0.5 mark.** The answer never expressly says that the informed shareholder approval is an **ordinary resolution**. It discusses a possible separate special-reserved-power direction under the articles, which is a different issue. The KAP and Module 8 section 5.1 require the liability-protection approval itself to be by ordinary resolution, unless the articles demand a supermajority.

2. **Application/voting loss: 0.75 mark.** The answer says that CSP (Nominees) Ltd should obtain all three beneficial owners' written instructions and makes the upstream nominee-board resolution conditional on all three. On three equal shares, the KAP's required point is that informed instructions from **Mr Green and Mrs Brown** give the two-share majority needed for the ordinary resolution. Miss Red is the purchaser and beneficiary. Her instruction may sensibly be disclosed and retained, and the later KAP illustration inconsistently refers to all three, but it is not required to make the ordinary majority. The answer therefore overstates the voting condition as practical unanimity and misses the Green/Brown point. Credit remains for correctly separating registered-member voting from beneficial-owner instructions.

3. **Drafting/precedent-fidelity loss: 0.75 mark.** The Company director resolution is substantively effective, but it does not track the KAP's Appendix 25M-derived architecture as strictly as it should. In particular:

   - the KAP separately records that the Assignment is attached and forms part of the resolutions; the answer refers to a draft produced and later marked `A`, but does not plainly say that a copy is attached and forms part;
   - the KAP separates the operative decisions into (1) Company shall assign and sell, (2) the Assignment's terms are approved and (3) the Assignment is entered into and executed;
   - the answer merges the first two effects into its first resolution, then adds execution, ancillary-action and record-keeping resolutions.

   All core legal effects remain present, so this is a limited architecture deduction rather than a finding that the draft is invalid.

There are no other mark deductions. The same defect is not counted twice merely because the all-three-instructions condition appears in both the prose and an upstream resolution.

## Extra upstream corporate-actor resolutions

The separate board resolutions for CSP (Nominees) Ltd and CSP (Directors) Ltd are **mostly mark-neutral**.

- They demonstrate awareness that a corporate member and corporate director act through authorised humans. That is procedurally careful and consistent with the local Routing v2 document-chain rule.
- The KAP expects only Company's member resolution and Company's director resolution. The upstream documents therefore do not earn additional marks once actor and signatory authority have been recognised.
- They do not replace or confuse the two required Company resolutions because the answer labels and sequences all four documents clearly. No separate deduction is made for including them.
- They are inefficient in a 12-mark examination sub-part and depend on unknown board compositions and constitutions. Their practical cost is time and length. The nominee-board resolution also carries the all-three-instructions error already deducted above.

The better exam treatment would be a short preliminary statement that each corporate actor must authorise its human signatory, followed by the two requested Company resolutions, unless the examiner expressly asks for the upstream instruments.

## KAP errors and inconsistencies: no penalty to the answer

The answer correctly departs from several slips in the KAP illustration:

- The paper says the purchaser is **Miss Red**. The KAP draft repeatedly says **Mr Red**. The answer consistently uses Miss Red and must not be penalised.
- The paper identifies the asset as an interest in the **XYZ Private Equity Partnership**. The KAP illustration sometimes calls it the XYZ Private Equity Fund. The answer uses the paper's Partnership terminology.
- The KAP later says instructions are given by `Messrs Green, Brown and Red`, although the paper identifies Mr Green, Miss Red and Mrs Brown. The answer uses the correct names and honorifics.
- The KAP member recital refers to the board proceeding without liability for `their` duty even though Company has one corporate Sole Director. The answer correctly uses `its` duty.
- The KAP's third director resolution says the Assignment is executed by or on behalf of Sole Director. The answer more accurately authorises a human acting for CSP (Directors) Ltd, in its capacity as Sole Director, to execute the Assignment for Company.

The KAP itself is also internally inconsistent on instructions: its prose says Green and Brown are needed to pass the ordinary resolution, whereas the later director recital mentions instructions from Green, Brown and Red. The answer receives credit for recognising all three nominee relationships, but the exact majority analysis still needed to say that Green and Brown supply the necessary two votes and Red's instruction is optional for threshold purposes.

## Highest-value fixes

1. State expressly that full disclosure is followed by an **ordinary resolution** of Company, subject to any higher threshold in the actual articles.
2. Explain the voting arithmetic: CSP (Nominees) Ltd casts the votes as registered member; informed instructions for Green's and Brown's two shares are sufficient for the ordinary majority. Record Red's purchaser interest and any instruction she gives, but do not make her instruction a condition of the majority.
3. Rebuild the Company sole-director draft around Appendix 25M's component sequence: transaction; Assignment attached and forming part; prior member approval; independent best-interests decision; then separate resolutions to assign/sell, approve the terms and enter into/execute the Assignment.
4. Keep Appendix 23C's core three-recital architecture compact. Extra valuation detail can be incorporated within its risks recital without creating a new architecture.
5. Reduce the two upstream corporate-authority instruments to a brief signatory-authority caveat unless the question specifically requests them. That preserves time for the two drafts the KAP awards marks for.
